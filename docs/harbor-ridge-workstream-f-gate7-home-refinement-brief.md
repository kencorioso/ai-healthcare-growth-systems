## Overall Diagnosis

Your instinct is correct, and it's worth naming precisely *why* the page reads as a case study rather than a premium experience: **every section currently has the same visual density and the same rhythm.** Heading, paragraph, maybe one bordered box, repeat — eight times in a row. ## Section-by-Section Assessment

### Hero + Video 1 — **REFINE**

The two-zone structure is correct and stays. The positioning statement is the primary above-the-fold asset and must retain greater visual authority than the video — not an equal split, and not a video-dominant split.

**Decision:** The text zone receives greater horizontal territory than the video zone on desktop — conceptually around a 58/42 or 60/40 relationship, exact ratio browser-refined. This restores genuinely commanding Hero typography rather than compressing the headline to accommodate an enlarged video.

Hero copy and Video 1 must still read as one integrated composition, not two independent rectangles placed side by side — that requirement is unchanged. But the specific mechanism is now governed by intent, not a mandated device: proportion, alignment, and a restrained connective treatment should achieve the integration. A literal horizontal connective rule is a legitimate option, not a requirement — use one only if browser inspection confirms it genuinely strengthens the composition rather than functioning as decoration for its own sake.

### The Problem — **REFINE**

Correctly plain prose per the brief's editorial treatment — this should not become a card. But "plain" has tipped into "undifferentiated" — it currently looks like a Word document paragraph, not an authored editorial moment.

**Decision:** Give this section one deliberate typographic device that signals "this is the founding tension of the whole page" without adding a container: set the opening clause ("Harbor Ridge Behavioral Health started with a disagreement...") at the lead-paragraph size (`--text-lead`) for the first sentence only, dropping to standard body size for the rest. This is a Stripe-style device — using scale, not boxes, to mark editorial importance — and costs nothing in terms of new components.

### What I Built / Golden Thread — **RECOMPOSE**

This is currently six bordered rectangles in a row with arrows between them. It reads as a row of buttons or a breadcrumb trail, not a visual argument about cross-silo visibility — the brief's own stated purpose for this object is not being achieved by the current execution, even though it technically satisfies "chain, not diagram."

**Decision:** Replace the six equal boxes with a single connecting line (a literal thread, rendered as a thin horizontal rule in Slate Border or Sovereign Navy) running beneath all six stage labels, with each stage marked by a small node (a dot or short vertical tick) sitting on the line rather than a bordered container around the label. This makes the *connection itself* the visual subject — the thing that's actually novel about Harbor Ridge (one continuous evidence chain across departments that normally don't talk to each other) — rather than six isolated labels that happen to have arrows between them.

**Added domain dimension:** the six stages carry restrained, metadata-level domain labels grouping them by organizational function, integrated into the same thread composition, not as separate cards or a second diagram:

- **MARKETING** — Acquisition, Inquiry
- **ADMISSIONS** — Financial Qualification, Admission
- **CLINICAL** — Treatment
- **REVENUE CYCLE** — Revenue

These domain labels sit as small typographic markers (Inter, metadata scale, per Brand & Voice v2.0's existing metadata treatment — not the chip/pill treatment, which is reserved for genuine categorical labels like Observed Finding) positioned above or alongside their associated stage nodes on the thread — grouping certain nodes visually without breaking the single continuous line or introducing a fourth visual layer. The composition must still read as one thread; the domain labels are annotation on that thread, not a parallel structure. This is what makes the object communicate its actual strategic point — one evidence chain crossing organizational boundaries that don't normally share visibility — rather than just showing six sequential steps.

### What It Found / $251K — **RECOMPOSE**

You named this exactly right: it's prose inside a tinted box, with the number as one clause in a sentence. This is the single most important number on the page and it currently has less visual presence than the section heading above it.

**Decision:** Extract the number. "$251,000" becomes a standalone, large-scale Jost numeral — genuinely the largest piece of type on this section, larger than the H2 above it — set on its own line, with "OBSERVED FINDING" as a small chip positioned *above* the number (a caption, not a buried mid-sentence label), and the qualifying sentence ("an estimated... additional collections if out-of-network claims had realized at the same rate as in-network ones") set as smaller supporting text *beneath* the number.

**Added secondary evidence:** the two realization rates already supported by the approved copy and Evidence Trail data — INN collection realization: 66.77%, OON collection realization: 30.38% — appear within the same composition as secondary evidence, subordinate in scale to the $251,000 headline figure. These are not a dashboard and not new analytical content; they exist to let the viewer immediately see the quantitative basis for the finding without needing to click through. Treatment: two smaller supporting numerals (comparable in weight to the qualifying sentence, clearly secondary to the $251,000 figure), labeled plainly (e.g., "INN realization" / "OON realization"), positioned near the qualifying sentence rather than competing with the headline number for primary visual attention.

The Evidence Trail link remains the path to the complete arithmetic, unchanged — this composition shows the basis for the finding, not the full derivation.
