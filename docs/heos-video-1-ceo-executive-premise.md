# HEOS Evidence Engine — Video 1: CEO / Executive Premise

**Status:** Workstream E — Final / Freeze-Ready (persuasion-pass version)
**Target length:** approximately 95–110 seconds

## Full Script

Most executives don't have a data problem. They have a connection problem.

Marketing knows what generated the inquiry. Admissions knows what happened to the opportunity. Operations knows what happened after someone became a patient. Finance knows whether the revenue ever materialized.

But leadership needs to understand the whole chain.

I spent years inside behavioral healthcare marketing and operations watching those functions hold different pieces of the same story. That's the problem that led me here.

HEOS is my broader vision for connecting those signals into better executive intelligence. The first capability I've built toward that vision is the HEOS Evidence Engine, an AI reasoning system for evidence-based executive performance analysis.

I built it to investigate a deceptively simple question:

Where is performance actually breaking down, and what evidence supports that conclusion?

To test that capability rigorously, I built Harbor Ridge Behavioral Health, a synthetic 32-bed treatment facility designed as an evaluation testbed. Harbor Ridge let me test and characterize the Evidence Engine's capabilities and limitations under controlled blind conditions.

Here's one Observed Finding it surfaced: an estimated $251,000 additional collections opportunity if out-of-network claims had realized at the in-network rate.

That was a genuine pattern in the data. But it was not a Benchmark Result, and it was not one of the deliberately hidden problems the Evidence Engine was being tested to find.

I designed the Evidence Engine to trace findings like that through what I call the Golden Thread: traceability from acquisition to inquiry, financial qualification, admission, treatment, and ultimately revenue.

That's the real opportunity. The goal isn't another dashboard. It's to reason across information that normally lives in separate organizational silos and make the supporting evidence inspectable.

But useful discovery isn't the same thing as reliable diagnosis.

When I blind-tested the Evidence Engine against hidden problems whose answers had been frozen in advance, it did not recover either hidden problem.

It would have been easy to keep adjusting the test until I got the result I wanted. I didn't. I kept the failures in the record, because a system I can't trust to fail honestly isn't one I'd trust with real numbers.

The goal is to help executives see across organizational silos, identify where performance is actually breaking down, and inspect the evidence before trusting the conclusion.

## The One Sentence the Viewer Should Remember
