## Full Script

Most executives don't have a data problem. They have a connection problem.

Here's what that looks like in practice: admissions are declining, even as marketing spend and inquiry volume go up. Marketing believes admissions isn't converting the demand they're generating. Admissions believes marketing is sending lower-quality opportunities.

Both sides have real evidence. Neither has the whole picture. And leadership can't confidently decide what's actually happening until it can see how those pieces connect.

I spent years inside behavioral healthcare marketing and operations, watching people in different departments reach genuinely different conclusions about the same business — because each side only had part of the picture. That's the problem that led me here.

So I built the HEOS Evidence Engine, an AI reasoning system designed to trace evidence across those organizational boundaries, rather than rely on any one department's version of events. I tested it against Harbor Ridge, a synthetic healthcare facility built specifically to evaluate it under controlled conditions.

It surfaced a real finding: an estimated $251,000 additional collections opportunity if out-of-network claims had realized at the in-network rate. But when I tested it against problems I'd deliberately hidden for it to find, it missed both.

I kept that result — because an evaluation only tells you something real if you're willing to keep the parts that don't go your way.

The point isn't that I built an interesting piece of software. It's that Marketing and Admissions were both right about what they were seeing — they just couldn't see it together. I built the Evidence Engine to help me investigate across that gap more rigorously, not to replace the judgment it still takes to make sense of what's found.
